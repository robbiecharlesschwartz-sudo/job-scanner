How to read a card
Strong
You meet every stated requirement and it's squarely your target: an entry-level strategy, management or sustainability consulting role, or in-house corporate strategy, based in NYC.
What to do: Apply now and put it first. Look for a referral, tailor your resume to the posting, and lead with the GDI ESG work.
Good
You meet the hard requirements, but it's a step to the side of your core lanes (operations or transformation consulting, a rotational program, strategic finance, business development), or one detail isn't confirmed.
What to do: Worth applying. Tailor your resume to the angle it emphasises; slightly less urgent than Strong.
Stretch
Realistic but a reach: one thing sits a little above you, like ~2 years asked for, a Consultant title with no stated level, or a specialised industry.
What to do: Apply if you have time, ideally after networking with someone on the team. Lead with transferable proof (the GDI procurement and AI tool); expect lower odds.
No
Fails a hard requirement in your profile (location, level, degree, start date or off-target work). Never shown on the page.

The rating is about the role. Under each job: ✓ where you fit, ✗ a gap or risk, ~ something the posting leaves unclear. A role that fails a hard requirement is never shown, however good the company.

Company fit (High / Medium / Low / Unknown) is separate: it weighs learning, employee support, how distinctive the work is and how plausible a genuine interest is (weights in preferences.toml). It only orders matches within the same rating. Unknown means too little evidence, not a bad company.

Evidence labels

  • Verified policy the company documents it (a policy or program exists - not proof every team practises it)
  • Supporting evidence credible independent context about how it works in practice
  • Tentative signal indirect, partial or self-reported - treat as a lead to check
  • Unknown no reliable evidence either way; never counted against a company
